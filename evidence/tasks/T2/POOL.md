# POOL — distinct verified claims about what happens when the Northfield cutover plan is executed

1. [calendar-overruns-deadline] MECHANISM: Six weeks starting Monday 6 October puts cutover on Friday 7 November and hypercare on 10–14 November, against a stated constraint of "finished by end of October". CONSEQUENCE: The plan misses its own agreed deadline by roughly two weeks on the day it is written, before any execution risk materialises.

2. [golive-lands-on-peak-monday] MECHANISM: Go-live Monday is 10 November and the plan itself names the second week of November as the start of peak season, so hypercare week is peak week one. CONSEQUENCE: The site runs its newest, least stable process at its highest volume of the year, with no quiet period in which to absorb defects.

3. [late-discovery-triggers-compression] MECHANISM: The calendar collision surfaces only when someone has to write a real date on a form — a room booking, the temp's engagement, the cutover night — around Weeks 3–4, and peak season cannot move, so the schedule is recovered by compression rather than by slipping. CONSEQUENCE: Compression lands on the parallel run and the training, because they are the only weeks with no fixed physical event attached, so the plan sacrifices its only verification and readiness activities and keeps everything that consumes and delivers.

4. [cutover-pulled-forward-variant] MECHANISM: An alternative response to the deadline discovery is to pull the cutover forward to Friday 31 October to honour "end of October". CONSEQUENCE: Training week and cutover week collapse into one, Week 5 disappears as a buffer, and a five-day-old system enters peak with staff who received the scanners days earlier.

5. [deadline-passes-without-decision] MECHANISM: 31 October arrives with the site still entirely on the legacy system and nothing final exported, but the decision to continue was already taken during the Week 3 gate. CONSEQUENCE: The visible, undeniable deadline miss generates no course correction and becomes a footnote in a status report.

6. [export-has-no-button] MECHANISM: "Export the current stock table to CSV" on a fifteen-year-old green-screen Oracle application requires credentials and schema knowledge that live only with a contractor who cannot come on site. CONSEQUENCE: The export consumes one to two of the three survey days and/or waits on multi-day contractor round trips, squeezing the interviews and the write-up.

7. [export-is-a-report-not-a-table] MECHANISM: What is actually obtainable is a spooled report or canned stock listing rather than the underlying table — page headers repeated every few lines, fixed-width space padding, fields truncated to the report's column width, thousands separators, a grand-total row. CONSEQUENCE: The file parses without error while carrying structurally damaged location codes and permanently truncated fields, so it fails silently.

8. [loud-defects-caught-silent-ones-not] MECHANISM: The export defects that are visible in the first fifty lines (page furniture, headers, obvious truncation) get found and fixed; the ones that are silent (padding, encoding, numeric coercion, shifted fields) do not. CONSEQUENCE: Fixing the visible defects creates the impression that the export has been scrutinised, which suppresses any further inspection of the silent ones.

9. [unquoted-commas-shift-fields] MECHANISM: Free-text descriptions accumulated over fifteen years contain commas, and a hand-rolled spool does not quote them, so every field to the right shifts on those rows. CONSEQUENCE: The location column of those rows fills with prose fragments that are then counted as "malformed location codes", inflating the 4% figure and permanently hiding the true cause.

10. [numeric-coercion-of-sku-keys] MECHANISM: SKU columns held as Oracle NUMBER, or opened and saved in a spreadsheet, lose leading zeros and render long codes in rounded scientific notation. CONSEQUENCE: The SKU key silently stops matching the barcodes physically on the stock, breaks the join back to the Week 5 export, and collapses genuinely distinct codes into one value that the dedupe then merges.

11. [excel-date-coercion] MECHANISM: The hand-fixing will be done in a spreadsheet, and location codes of the form `3-12` or `03-05-02` are exactly date-shaped, so they are converted to serial date numbers on save. CONSEQUENCE: An unknown subset of location strings is irreversibly destroyed and replaced with plausible-looking numbers that raise no error at any later stage.

12. [character-set-mismatch] MECHANISM: The legacy database is US7ASCII or ISO-8859 and the downstream toolchain assumes UTF-8, and nobody proof-reads tens of thousands of descriptions. CONSEQUENCE: Item descriptions arrive permanently mangled, and where a character is dropped rather than substituted two previously distinct SKU strings become identical and get merged by the dedupe.

13. [stock-table-is-many-tables] MECHANISM: "The current stock table" is singular but the migration needs item master, unit-of-measure conversions, lot/serial/expiry, and stock in non-nominal statuses — quarantine, damaged, customer returns, consignment — none of which the plan exports or decides. CONSEQUENCE: Stock that must never be sold is loaded as good pickable stock, so on go-live the platform allocates it, a picker picks it, and it ships to a customer.

14. [work-in-flight-not-migrated] MECHANISM: Open purchase orders, expected receipts, in-transit stock and part-picked orders appear nowhere in the export, the cleanup, or the Week 5 freeze. CONSEQUENCE: On Monday a lorry arrives against a PO that exists only in a read-only system, and a part-picked customer order exists as a tote on the floor and as nothing in the platform.

15. [allocations-not-migrated] MECHANISM: The export is a stock export, so quantities already allocated or reserved to orders in the legacy system do not cross. CONSEQUENCE: The platform shows committed stock as free and re-allocates from scratch on Monday morning, producing bin assignments that disagree with the paperwork already printed on Friday.

16. [uom-not-exported] MECHANISM: Pack conversions (each / inner / case of 12 / pallet of 480) live in a table nobody exports, and the platform's mandatory UoM field gets filled with a global default such as EACH to make the load pass. CONSEQUENCE: Quantities for every case- or pallet-held item are wrong by one to three orders of magnitude, and the wrong number looks entirely unremarkable in the data.

17. [no-control-totals-recorded] MECHANISM: No row count, total units, SKU count or stock value is ever recorded from the legacy system's own summary and carried through the export, the dedupe, the staging load, the Week 3 comparison and the production load. CONSEQUENCE: Every omission-class error — a truncated export, a keep-first dedupe, a partial load — survives every gate, because each gate's chosen instrument reports on rows that are present and is blind to rows that are simply not there.

18. [three-shifts-versus-day-engineers] MECHANISM: Two day-working engineers on site for three days must interview three supervisors who sit on three different shifts. CONSEQUENCE: One or two interviews compress into a twenty-minute handover conversation or a phone call, and the shift whose work is least like the day shift is the one least documented.

19. [informal-layer-not-captured] MECHANISM: Asked how they work, fifteen-year supervisors describe the documented process, because the undocumented layer — the floating location code for anything that doesn't fit, the pallet left at the end of an aisle, the marker pen on the shelf edge, the notebook in the drawer — is not "the process" to them, it is just the job. CONSEQUENCE: The migration is specified against the official warehouse while the building runs on the unofficial one, which makes the migration wrong in places where the data itself was clean.

20. [new-supervisor-overweighted] MECHANISM: The supervisor who started in August is the most available and most willing interviewee and describes the process fluently as it is supposed to work, while the veterans' accounts arrive in fragments between tasks. CONSEQUENCE: The write-up over-represents the shift with the least institutional knowledge, and second-hand explanations of legacy conventions are recorded with the same weight as first-hand ones.

21. [no-picker-interviewed] MECHANISM: The survey speaks only to the three supervisors, while a third of the pickers joined within six months and learned the job from each other rather than from management. CONSEQUENCE: The process the survey records may not be the process a third of the floor actually runs.

22. [writeup-never-validated] MECHANISM: The write-up is the survey's only durable artefact, is finished off-site after the engineers have left, and is never circulated back to the supervisors it describes for correction. CONSEQUENCE: Its errors are permanent from the day it is written, and every decision in Weeks 2–5 is made from it by people who are no longer in the building.

23. [supervisor-knowledge-needed-after-access-ends] MECHANISM: Supervisor knowledge is collected in Week 1 as open interviews, before anyone knows which questions matter, and the moment those questions become specific is Week 2 — when the engineers are off site, the supervisors are running shifts, and nobody has been released from shift duties. CONSEQUENCE: The hand-fixing of location codes, the task that most requires their knowledge, is done by engineers inferring answers instead.

24. [supervisors-as-sources-not-owners] MECHANISM: The supervisors are consulted once as sources and are never asked to review the write-up, validate the mapping, deliver training, or take part in the go/no-go. CONSEQUENCE: They surface no problems proactively ("nobody asked me") and voice their scepticism downward to their teams rather than upward to the project, which is where the 40 staff form their opinion.

25. [no-physical-measurement-before-freeze] MECHANISM: Nothing in Weeks 1–4 compares any record to a physical shelf; the plan's only physical verification is the Saturday spot count, which happens after the legacy system is frozen. CONSEQUENCE: The one variable that decides the outcome — whether stock is actually where the record says — stays unmeasured until after the last reversible moment has passed.

26. [oracle-box-is-a-single-point-of-failure] MECHANISM: The entire migration, and the implicit fallback, depend on one unredundant, out-of-support machine maintained four days a year, with backup state unknown and no step anywhere in the plan to verify a restore or take a cold copy. CONSEQUENCE: If it fails at any point in the six weeks the project has no data source and no recovery path, and the physical stock in the building becomes the only record.

27. [dedupe-collapses-multibin-rows] MECHANISM: A stock file keyed on SKU-plus-location legitimately holds one row per SKU per bin, so "dedupe SKUs" applied with any standard tool deletes the non-first rows or sums them into one. CONSEQUENCE: Either total on-hand drops sharply and the surviving location is arbitrary, or the totals reconcile perfectly while every multi-bin SKU now claims to be entirely in one bin, which is exactly the error the Week 3 comparison cannot see.

28. [dedupe-merges-distinct-skus] MECHANISM: Case, whitespace and suffix variants are folded together, but some legacy conventions overload case or a trailing character with meaning — an obsolete revision, an alternate supplier. CONSEQUENCE: Two genuinely different parts become one record whose stock is the sum of two incompatible things, permanently and invisibly.

29. [semantic-duplicates-survive] MECHANISM: The real duplication — the same physical part created twice over fifteen years under two unrelated codes — cannot be detected by string matching and requires part knowledge nobody on the project has. CONSEQUENCE: The platform goes live holding both codes for the same item, often in the same bin, generating a permanent replenishment demand for one and a permanent over-count on the other that nobody is scheduled to reconcile.

30. [no-sku-crosswalk] MECHANISM: The dedupe is performed as in-place edits to a file, so no `old_code → new_code` mapping table is produced. CONSEQUENCE: Every physical label in the building bearing a merged-away code becomes unresolvable when scanned, order lines and POs referencing it point at nothing, and the merge cannot be audited or reversed after the fact because the losing rows were deleted rather than recorded.

31. [four-percent-is-an-unverified-estimate] MECHANISM: The 4% comes verbally and from memory from a contractor who sees the system four days a year, is never re-derived from the actual file, and describes only codes that visibly fail to parse. CONSEQUENCE: The largest hand-work task in the project is budgeted against a number nobody measured, and the number is written into the plan where it hardens from an estimate into a commitment.

32. [silent-misparse-class-is-bigger] MECHANISM: Beyond the codes that fail to parse there is a larger class that parses cleanly and means something else — inconsistent zero-padding producing `012` and `12` as distinct bays, undelimited codes with two valid readings, half-bay side characters folded away, tribal suffixes stripped, aisles renumbered but never relabelled. CONSEQUENCE: The rate of rows whose loaded location does not correspond to physical reality is several times the stated 4%, raises no error at any stage, and is never counted because nothing in the plan counts it.

33. [non-rack-locations-have-no-model] MECHANISM: Valid legacy locations such as goods-in, despatch, quarantine, returns, bulk floor, mezzanine, yard and blank are operational states encoded in a location field, and a three-field aisle-bay-level schema has nowhere to put them. CONSEQUENCE: They are either flagged as malformed, inflating the reject count, or forced through a positional parser into valid-looking bins such as aisle `BULK` that correspond to nothing in the building and are invisible until a picker is sent to one.

34. [parser-loosened-to-hit-4-percent] MECHANISM: The first validator run flags far more than 4%, and because 4% is the agreed number the response is to loosen the parser until the reject count comes back down rather than revise the estimate. CONSEQUENCE: Loud, countable, recoverable failures are converted into silent, plausible, wrong mappings that validate, load, and point at real bins holding something else.

35. [handfix-does-not-fit-the-week] MECHANISM: Hundreds to thousands of rows each needing a lookup, a question to a busy supervisor, or a walk to the rack cannot be resolved by two engineers who are simultaneously deduping, mapping, and standing up the staging load in one week. CONSEQUENCE: The hand-fix is replaced by a blanket rule and a catch-all destination, so the residue enters the platform with correct quantities and no findable location.

36. [catch-all-bucket-detonates-at-the-pick-face] MECHANISM: The catch-all bucket is created with an honest intention to resolve it during the parallel run, but the parallel run does not touch locations and nobody counts or caps the bucket. CONSEQUENCE: The platform reports available stock that the floor cannot retrieve, so the site short-ships items that are sitting on a shelf.

37. [desk-inference-concentrates-errors] MECHANISM: Repaired from a desk without ground truth, the only available heuristic is adjacency to neighbouring rows, which works for dense, well-organised, frequently-touched stock and fails for everything else. CONSEQUENCE: The residual errors are correlated rather than randomly distributed, and they concentrate in exactly the population that the parallel run and the Saturday spot count will not sample, which defeats the sampling logic of both.

38. [fixes-are-edits-not-rules] MECHANISM: "We will fix those by hand" produces edits attached to the Week 1 file rather than a mapping table plus a transformation script, and the legacy system itself is never corrected — only the extract is. CONSEQUENCE: The Week 5 export from the same uncorrected source brings every malformed code back unfixed, and the week's hand work cannot be re-run on cutover night.

39. [location-master-does-not-exist] MECHANISM: The platform models locations as entities with their own constraints, and stock cannot be loaded into a bin that does not exist, but nothing in the plan creates Northfield's racking in the platform. CONSEQUENCE: The staging load rejects wholesale on referential validation, and the rest of Week 2 is consumed building a bin list that was never a scheduled deliverable.

40. [location-master-built-from-data-not-building] MECHANISM: The bin list gets generated from the distinct location values found in the file, or auto-created on import, rather than from a survey of the racking — the only physical record being three days of photographs taken by people who had never been in the building. CONSEQUENCE: Every mis-parse is canonised as a real bin, bins that were empty in Week 1 are missing, bins that no longer exist are created, and these phantoms live permanently in replenishment and cycle-count queues with no report capable of identifying them.

41. [lexical-sort-breaks-pick-path] MECHANISM: Pick sequence is derived from the location fields, and three string fields with inconsistent zero-padding sort lexically — bay 1, 10, 11, 12, 2, 20 — while the walking route encoded in the legacy numbering scheme is carried in no migrated field. CONSEQUENCE: Every pick list is in a bad order even where the data is perfectly correct, pickers walk the building several times per round, and it presents as a design complaint rather than a bug, so nothing logs it and no data fix addresses it.

42. [mandatory-fields-bulk-defaulted] MECHANISM: The platform's required-field set was shaped by four other sites and demands fields the legacy extract does not contain, and the only way to make the file load inside the week is to fill them with constants. CONSEQUENCE: Whole product groups acquire wrong categories, owners and storage attributes that look normal in the data and misdirect the platform's own logic from day one.

43. [shared-item-master-collision] MECHANISM: Nobody establishes whether the platform's item catalogue is shared across the five sites before loading fifteen years of Northfield codes into it. CONSEQUENCE: If it is shared, the load either fails on duplicate keys or attaches Northfield stock to another site's item record, and Northfield's duplicate and dead codes pollute the master for four live warehouses — noticed by people at other sites who report it as their own problem.

44. [staging-load-overruns-invisibly] MECHANISM: The staging load is an iteration loop of submit, error report, trace back through the dedupe and mapping, fix, resubmit — not an event — so Week 2 does not finish in Week 2; and Week 3's activity is performed by pickers and a temp, not by the engineers. CONSEQUENCE: The engineers' slip is absorbed by a week that does not need them, no status line ever reads red, and nobody re-baselines the schedule.

45. [partial-load-reported-as-success] MECHANISM: A row-by-row loader reports "18,400 of 20,000 rows loaded" and writes the rest to a reject log that gets skimmed under time pressure, and the rejects are not a random sample — they are the odd units of measure, the non-rack locations, the held stock and the long descriptions. CONSEQUENCE: A load that dropped a material percentage of the hardest and most valuable rows is carried into the status update as "the load succeeded", and no later step compares counts.

46. [staging-does-not-predict-production] MECHANISM: Staging has stale configuration, different or relaxed validation, no printer bindings, no scanner clients, no integrations and possibly a stale catalogue, so a clean staging load proves the file is parseable and satisfies staging's constraints. CONSEQUENCE: The first genuine production load happens for the first time on cutover night, and at least one failure appears that staging never showed, in the window, at night.

47. [one-temp-one-shift-of-three] MECHANISM: A single temp works one shift at a site running three, so two of every three operating hours have no re-keyer at all. CONSEQUENCE: Late- and night-shift transactions are batched into the following morning or never entered, which breaks "within the hour" by construction rather than by accident.

48. [volume-exceeds-keying-rate] MECHANISM: A 40-person warehouse in pre-peak build-up generates transactions in the low thousands per day, against a sustained manual transcription rate of a few hundred per shift. CONSEQUENCE: The temp is behind by Monday lunchtime and the backlog compounds every hour, because the arrival rate exceeds the service rate on every shift.

49. [temp-batches-and-skips] MECHANISM: Unable to keep pace, the temp stops transcribing individual transactions and starts entering net movements per SKU at end of shift, skipping the small, repetitive or ambiguous ones. CONSEQUENCE: The parallel run stops testing whether platform transactions behave like legacy ones and tests arithmetic only, and the skipped population is biased toward exactly the awkward rows most likely to be wrong.

50. [invisible-shortcuts-survive-to-friday] MECHANISM: Shortcuts that make the numbers diverge get noticed and corrected during the week, while shortcuts that make the numbers agree — end-of-shift batching, taking a shift summary from a supervisor, adjusting the platform to match legacy when they disagree — produce no signal. CONSEQUENCE: Selection pressure runs one way all week, so the practices that survive to Friday are precisely the ones that manufacture agreement.

51. [nobody-owns-the-temp] MECHANISM: The plan names no supervisor for the temp, no definition of what counts as "a transaction", no backlog measure, no accuracy sampling and no induction — and the site's own staff are not trained on the platform until the following week, so nobody on site can check the work. CONSEQUENCE: An unsupervised person with no domain knowledge and an impossible target optimises for finishing the visible pile, which is the only strategy available to them.

52. [transcription-error-is-indistinguishable] MECHANISM: Manual keying of alphanumeric SKU and location codes runs at roughly one percent per field, with an error profile of 0/O, 1/I, and transpositions on strings made of nothing else. CONSEQUENCE: Hundreds of introduced errors accumulate in the platform that are indistinguishable, in the Friday comparison, from genuine migration errors.

53. [comparison-blind-to-location] MECHANISM: The Friday gate compares stock levels — quantity per SKU — while the dominant defect of this migration is locational. CONSEQUENCE: Both systems can agree perfectly that there are 240 of a SKU while the platform believes they are in aisle 12 and they are physically in aisle 4, so every location-mapping error passes the plan's only verification untouched.

54. [comparison-tests-the-wrong-delta] MECHANISM: The platform's opening balance is the Week 2 cleaned file, so any transformation error sits on both sides of the week's delta and cancels out. CONSEQUENCE: Comparing end-of-week platform against end-of-week legacy tests the week's re-keying rather than the migration.

55. [only-fast-movers-move] MECHANISM: In one week perhaps 15–30% of SKUs move, and they are the fast movers at the front of the building, at ground level, well labelled, whose real locations everyone already knows. CONSEQUENCE: The parallel run gives coverage to the part of the file that needed validating least and zero coverage to the back aisles, top levels, bulk floor and seasonal stock where the location data is oldest and worst.

56. [inner-join-hides-missing-rows] MECHANISM: The comparison needs a common key, but merged SKUs no longer exist under their legacy codes, and the easy comparison to build is an inner join or a lookup. CONSEQUENCE: Rows missing from the platform entirely are reported as zero discrepancies rather than as mismatches, which is the exact mechanism by which a partial load receives a clean bill of health.

57. [agree-has-no-threshold] MECHANISM: "If they agree we proceed" defines no tolerance, no pass criterion, no named decider and no action for disagreement, and the systems cannot agree exactly. CONSEQUENCE: A tolerance is invented in the room after seeing the numbers, which is not a gate, and the failure produces a meeting rather than a stop.

58. [variance-blamed-on-the-temp] MECHANISM: There are three candidate explanations for the discrepancy list — migration error, transcription error, and transactions never entered — and separating them takes work nobody has time for, while "it's the temp's keying and the backlog" is partly true, unfalsifiable, and permits proceeding under a deadline everyone now knows is blown. CONSEQUENCE: A mostly-correct explanation absorbs the whole discrepancy and licenses dismissing the fraction that is genuine mapping error, so the only checkpoint with authority to stop the cutover is spent.

59. [failing-the-gate-has-no-branch] MECHANISM: If the comparison is honestly failed there is no defined alternative: repeating the parallel run pushes cutover into peak week, fixing the discrepancies during training week uses the same unnamed people, and the third option is to proceed with a note. CONSEQUENCE: Finding the problem does not stop the plan; it converts into compression of the steps downstream, so the good branch converges back onto the main line.

60. [better-rekeying-makes-it-worse] MECHANISM: The obvious remedy for the temp's backlog — add a second keyer — improves agreement on quantities while the comparison remains structurally blind to location. CONSEQUENCE: The more competently the parallel run is executed, the more confidence it produces about the risk it cannot see.

61. [rekeying-taxes-the-floor] MECHANISM: Re-keying requires pickers to write down and hand over what they picked and to answer queries about it, adding work per pick during pre-peak build-up, in full view of forty people who watch a temp type every transaction twice. CONSEQUENCE: Throughput dips for the week and the floor forms its first substantive opinion of the platform — "it makes us do the job twice, and it needs more people" — before anyone has seen it.

62. [parallel-run-is-not-a-rehearsal] MECHANISM: Pickers scan into the legacy terminals exactly as usual, so nobody scans into the platform, prints a label from it, runs a pick list from it or confirms a despatch in it during the one week both systems are live. CONSEQUENCE: Every device, label and integration path still has zero hours of real use going into Week 4, and the week that could have been a dress rehearsal with the old system as a safety net builds no familiarity at all.

63. [temp-leaves-with-the-knowledge] MECHANISM: The temp's engagement ends on the Friday of Week 3, and nothing they learned about which transaction types were awkward, which SKUs never reconciled and which entries were never made is written down. CONSEQUENCE: The discrepancy list becomes permanently unresolvable, because the only person who knew what was missing has gone.

64. [scanners-arrive-unconfigured] MECHANISM: Forty-plus devices arrive on the Monday they are to be handed out and each needs charging, firmware, MDM enrolment, a WiFi profile and certificate, the platform client pointed at the right endpoint, symbology and suffix configuration and a user account — twenty to sixty minutes of hands-on work each, done by the same people delivering the training. CONSEQUENCE: The devices are not floor-ready for the first sessions, so training runs on slides or two demo units, and any delivery slip at all puts the first contact with a device at or after cutover.

65. [no-spares-or-batteries] MECHANISM: The device order will have matched the 40-person headcount, so there are no spare units, spare batteries or multi-bay cradles for a three-shift operation in which a dropped handheld is a routine event. CONSEQUENCE: Devices die mid-shift and every breakage takes a picker off the floor from the first week, with no pool to draw from.

66. [no-wifi-survey] MECHANISM: The site has never run modern handhelds, its wireless was specified for green screens and offices, no survey appears anywhere in the plan, and steel racking packed with stock at peak fill is the worst RF environment there is — coverage that holds in October changes as the racks fill in November. CONSEQUENCE: Aisle dead spots are discovered live, and the fix is a survey, access points, cabling and switch ports with a procurement and installation lead time measured in weeks, so it cannot be resolved before go-live.

67. [training-in-a-room-hides-dead-spots] MECHANISM: The likely training venue is a meeting room next to an access point, and if the devices are unprovisioned the training must be classroom-based anyway. CONSEQUENCE: The coverage problem is not discovered in training either, deleting the last opportunity to find the one fault that cannot be fixed after go-live.

68. [dead-spots-read-as-device-faults] MECHANISM: A coverage gap presents to a picker not as "no WiFi" but as a scanner that freezes intermittently in the middle of an aisle. CONSEQUENCE: The response is to swap the device, which changes nothing, and it is done more than once before anyone thinks about radio, so diagnosis is delayed while the symptom is attributed to the hardware.

69. [rescan-creates-duplicate-transactions] MECHANISM: When a scan appears not to register because the transaction is queuing, the picker does the human thing and scans again. CONSEQUENCE: Duplicate transactions produce double decrements and negative stock, so record accuracy diverges on a second, new axis that cannot be attributed to the data migration at all.

70. [rack-labels-encode-legacy-string] MECHANISM: Every rack label in the building encodes the legacy location as one barcode, while the platform models aisle, bay and level as three fields, and nothing in the plan produces a runtime alias table or a parse rule. CONSEQUENCE: Either the scan of a rack label fails on exactly the same non-canonical population the Week 2 mapping failed on, or thousands of labels must be reprinted and re-applied up ladders in working aisles — days of physical work that appear in no week of the plan and are discovered after there is time to do them.

71. [symbology-and-checkdigit-unspecified] MECHANISM: Legacy labels were printed years ago in whatever symbology the old system used, and nothing in the plan reconciles that with the new scanners' enabled symbologies and check-digit transmission settings. CONSEQUENCE: Either nothing scans at all and it presents as "the old labels are unreadable", or every scan is systematically off by a character, or an unchecked continuous symbology short-reads and silently returns a shorter, valid, wrong code.

72. [no-trainer-named-three-shifts] MECHANISM: Six half-day sessions across three shifts must run at roughly 06:00, 14:00 and 22:00, and the plan names nobody to deliver them — the engineers are scheduled on site only in Weeks 1 and 6. CONSEQUENCE: The three shifts do not receive equivalent training; the night shift gets a shortened session, a handout, or a cascade from a supervisor who received the training himself an hour earlier.

73. [floor-cover-not-agreed] MECHANISM: Taking 40 staff off the floor for half-days removes roughly 20–40 person-days of picking capacity from the week before peak, and nobody has agreed who covers it or paid for it. CONSEQUENCE: Attendance is materially incomplete and the selection is not random — supervisors release the people they can spare, so the busiest shift and the most critical staff are the ones who miss training.

74. [second-session-is-the-one-cut] MECHANISM: When the sessions have to be trimmed, session one is "how to pick and put away" and session two is "what to do when it goes wrong" — exceptions, short picks, damages, corrections, how to report a problem — which is the one that sounds skippable to someone who needs vans loaded this afternoon. CONSEQUENCE: Forty people arrive at go-live trained for the situation that will not happen and untrained for the one that will, so every exception in hypercare routes to a supervisor or an engineer.

75. [training-cannot-use-the-building] MECHANISM: The plan states there is no test environment mirroring Northfield's racking, so training necessarily teaches screens and button sequences against staging data rather than "scan the bin you will scan tomorrow". CONSEQUENCE: The first time anyone scans a real rack label with a real device in a real aisle is go-live morning, and training-room competence overstates floor competence by a margin the training feedback cannot reveal.

76. [staging-data-teaches-distrust] MECHANISM: Training against the Week 2 data sends trainees to bins where the item is not, and the available response is "ignore that, it's test data". CONSEQUENCE: Forty people are taught on day one that the system's locations are not to be trusted, which destroys the feedback loop that was the only realistic way to find the real errors — after cutover a picker who finds an empty bin shrugs instead of reporting it.

77. [retention-gap] MECHANISM: Training runs 27–31 October and first real use is 10 November, with no practice in between because the legacy system remains the live system until the evening of the 7th and there is nowhere else to practise. CONSEQUENCE: Procedural detail decays across ten to fourteen days, so functionally every one of the 40 is a first-time user on the first morning of peak.

78. [trained-cohort-decays] MECHANISM: A third of the team joined in the last six months and the churn continues at the same rate. CONSEQUENCE: Several of the 40 trained in October have left within weeks of go-live, and the loss concentrates in the newest cohort — the people who most needed the training.

79. [no-materials-no-onboarding-path] MECHANISM: The training is a one-off project event with no owner, no designated site trainer, no quick-reference card and no repeatable course. CONSEQUENCE: Every replacement and every new starter learns by watching whoever is nearest, which propagates the workarounds invented during hypercare rather than the process, until a majority of the floor has never received any formal training on the platform.

80. [seasonal-headcount-never-trained] MECHANISM: Peak season means the site takes on agency and seasonal staff, who arrive in November — after the only training window has closed, after the legacy system is gone, and around the week the engineers leave. CONSEQUENCE: The operating population at go-live is larger than and different from the trained population, and the newcomers are inducted by supervisors who are themselves days into the system, being taught the shadow process as if it were the process.

81. [new-pickers-amplify-data-errors] MECHANISM: A veteran given a wrong location shrugs, thinks "that'll be in the overflow", and finds it in ninety seconds; a six-week picker either stops dead and calls a supervisor or takes the nearest plausible thing from the adjacent bin. CONSEQUENCE: The identical data error produces a several-times larger operational failure at Northfield than at a stable site, and it produces mis-picks that reach customers while corrupting two stock figures at once.

82. [no-super-user-layer] MECHANISM: Nobody is designated and prepared as an on-site expert, and there is no train-the-trainer step anywhere in the six weeks. CONSEQUENCE: When the engineers leave on 14 November there is zero residual capability, and the role defaults to three supervisors who were never asked, on top of running peak shifts.

83. [last-van-is-not-last-work] MECHANISM: "Friday evening after the last van leaves" is not the end of operations at a three-shift site — evening receiving, returns processing, night replenishment and half-complete terminal sessions all come after it. CONSEQUENCE: Physical movements happen that exist in the building and in neither system permanently, because the export has already been taken and there is no second bite.

84. [nightshift-standdown-leaves-faces-unreplenished] MECHANISM: Either the Friday and Saturday night shifts are stood down for the cutover or they work through it and contradict the freeze, and the decision is left to the last week and taken on cost grounds. CONSEQUENCE: If they are stood down, the replenishment that normally sets up Monday's pick faces does not happen, so go-live starts on unreplenished faces in the week before peak.

85. [freeze-has-no-mechanism] MECHANISM: "Freeze the legacy system" on green screens has no defined implementation and in practice means telling people not to type, which stops humans but not the box's overnight batch, replenishment calculation or EDI receipt poller. CONSEQUENCE: Anything keyed or processed after the export is lost, and the exported file stops matching the system it came from, destroying the only reference point available for reconciliation.

86. [final-export-second-ever-attempt] MECHANISM: The load-bearing extract is performed for only the second time ever, at night, on the critical path, by people who have already worked a full day, with the contractor unreachable. CONSEQUENCE: Every export defect that Week 1 did not catch fires here under time pressure with nobody present who understands the source system.

87. [export-not-reproducible] MECHANISM: The exact report, query, filters and typed parameters used in Week 1 are almost certainly not written down in a form that can be re-run identically. CONSEQUENCE: The Week 5 file differs subtly — column order, an obsolete-item filter, a sort order — which breaks the load script, changes the row population, or changes which row a keep-first rule keeps.

88. [handfix-reapplication-misses-changed-rows] MECHANISM: With the Week 2 corrections attached to the Week 1 file rather than expressed as rules, the only move that fits the window is to join them onto the new export by SKU and original location string. CONSEQUENCE: The join covers only rows whose key is unchanged, so it silently fails for precisely the rows that moved — the reslotted fast movers — and it fails altogether for any SKU whose key the dedupe or a numeric coercion altered.

89. [five-weeks-of-drift-unmapped] MECHANISM: The mapping was built against the Week 1 snapshot, and five weeks of pre-peak trading have since created new SKUs, filled locations that were empty in October, and introduced new improvised location strings the mapping has never seen. CONSEQUENCE: These rows either fail the load or fall through to a default, and under a Friday-night deadline with no hand-fixing capacity the resolution for anything unmappable is the catch-all bin, so the bucket at go-live is materially larger than it was in Week 2.

90. [no-abort-criteria-or-owner] MECHANISM: The plan states no cutover window, no abort time, no threshold, no runbook and no named person who owns the go/no-go; Week 3's "if they agree" is its only conditional and every step after it is declarative, ending with "Monday the site runs on the platform only". CONSEQUENCE: At 01:30 on Saturday morning, with tired people improvising and nobody empowered to stop, the only available answer is to continue, whatever the state of the load.

91. [read-only-is-not-a-rollback] MECHANISM: "Legacy stays powered on but read-only, in case we need to look something up" is a reference facility — you cannot resume operating on it, and reversing would mean making it writable again and reverse-keying everything already transacted in the platform, with the temp's engagement long over. CONSEQUENCE: Practical reversibility ends at the Friday freeze, which is before any evidence about whether the load worked exists, so the Saturday go/no-go is nominal and the people making it believe they have a choice they do not have.

92. [printer-cutover-unrehearsed] MECHANISM: "Switch the label printers over" is one clause containing an entire subsystem cutover — repoint each printer, register it in the platform, assign a template, print a test, iterate — executed at the end of the longest evening of the project with no rehearsal, when nothing prevented printing being cut over and tested in Week 3 or 4. CONSEQUENCE: It consumes hours nobody budgeted at the worst possible moment, and each iteration is a walk to a printer and a wasted label.

93. [serial-attached-printer-hard-stop] MECHANISM: A printer physically serial-attached to the Oracle box, which is how a system of that vintage does goods-out labelling, cannot be driven by the platform at all without new hardware. CONSEQUENCE: Discovering it at 02:30 on cutover night is an unrecoverable stop for that station, with no workaround available before Monday.

94. [dpi-mismatch-unscannable-labels] MECHANISM: Label templates authored for the other four sites' printers render at two-thirds or double size on a head of different resolution, which pushes the barcode's narrow-bar dimension outside what a scanner can resolve while the human-readable text still looks fine. CONSEQUENCE: The labels pass a visual check and simply do not scan, and the only test that would catch it — print one and scan it with an actual handheld — is nowhere in the plan.

95. [label-media-mismatch] MECHANISM: The site's stock of blank label media was bought for the legacy formats, and the platform's templates assume their own size, sensing and type. CONSEQUENCE: If the format differs, content prints offset or split, the printer feeds blanks hunting for a gap, and correcting it needs new media with a lead time of days to weeks — discovered on a Friday night and unfixable before Monday.

96. [carrier-labels-fail-downstream] MECHANISM: Despatch labels are validated externally by the carrier rather than on site, and nothing in the plan distinguishes internal labels from carrier labels. CONSEQUENCE: The vans leave on Monday, the parcels are rejected or manually sorted at the carrier hub, and the site learns of it on Tuesday at the earliest, after a full day of despatches has gone out wrong.

97. [printing-has-no-fallback] MECHANISM: A read-only legacy system cannot print, so the one subsystem being physically re-pointed on Friday night is precisely the one the fallback arrangement does not cover. CONSEQUENCE: If platform printing does not work on Monday the site has no printing capability at all, old or new, and a warehouse that cannot produce labels cannot despatch.

98. [spot-count-sample-too-small] MECHANISM: Two hundred items against roughly twenty to thirty thousand stock lines is a sample of about one percent. CONSEQUENCE: Even a perfectly random sample returns only a handful of discrepancies against a several-percent error rate, which reads to a skeleton crew on a Saturday morning as ordinary counting noise rather than as hundreds of wrong lines in the building.

99. [spot-count-samples-the-healthy-population] MECHANISM: A skeleton crew counts what is countable on a Saturday morning — front aisles, ground and eye level, fast movers, nothing needing a ladder or a forklift — which is the best-maintained part of the file, while the defects sit in bulk floor, mezzanine, yard, quarantine, top levels and the hand-fixed rows. CONSEQUENCE: The count systematically samples away from the errors and passes for reasons unrelated to whether the migration worked.

100. [quantity-count-cannot-see-location-error] MECHANISM: Told the system says forty of an item in a given bin, a counter who does not find them there does not record a variance — they assume they misread and ask a colleague, and a fifteen-year veteran says "no, that's in A14". CONSEQUENCE: They walk there, count forty, write down forty, the count reconciles, and the location error is absorbed by human knowledge and never recorded anywhere.

101. [list-driven-count-blind-to-omissions] MECHANISM: The count's direction is unspecified, and the easy one to organise is list-driven — take the platform's list and go and check it — rather than blind and shelf-driven. CONSEQUENCE: It is completely blind to stock that is physically on a shelf and absent from the platform entirely, which is exactly what a collapsing dedupe and a partial load produce.

102. [count-has-no-threshold] MECHANISM: No discrepancy rate is agreed in advance as the level that stops the go-live, and by Saturday morning legacy is frozen, the export is taken, the load is applied and the printers are re-pointed. CONSEQUENCE: The count cannot fail — whatever it finds, the answer at noon is "note it, fix it, go live Monday" — so it functions as authorisation rather than as a test.

103. [tired-crew-resolves-toward-match] MECHANISM: The Saturday crew is unnamed and unnumbered, quite possibly the same people who worked through Friday night, counting on overtime against a screen they were trained on ten days ago and barely know. CONSEQUENCE: They resolve every ambiguity in favour of "it matches", so the count under-reports variance for human reasons entirely independent of what was sampled, and a clean-looking result is worse than no count at all.

104. [overrun-cuts-the-count-when-needed-most] MECHANISM: If the cutover window overruns into Saturday daylight, the spot count is the item that gets shortened or skipped. CONSEQUENCE: The plan's only physical check is dropped in precisely the branch where the load was messiest and the check was most needed.

105. [day-one-throughput-halves] MECHANISM: Go-live productivity of 40–60% of normal for the first weeks is the ordinary outcome of a warehouse system cutover, and the plan makes no allowance for it — no reduced order intake, no extra headcount, no stock pre-built at the other four sites, no customer communication. CONSEQUENCE: The shortfall lands in a week when volume is climbing, so the shipping backlog grows by a fraction of a week's volume per day instead of draining.

106. [exception-queue-caps-output] MECHANISM: Every "the stock isn't where the system says" routes to the three or four people who can answer — two engineers and the supervisors — on top of their own work, and each instance stops a picker for five to fifteen minutes. CONSEQUENCE: That serialised queue, rather than any single technical fault, is what caps the site's output, and the people in it burn out fastest in the month they can least afford to.

107. [new-supervisor-cannot-absorb-errors] MECHANISM: The two veterans clear location exceptions from personal knowledge of where the stock actually is; the supervisor who started in August has no such knowledge to compensate with. CONSEQUENCE: His shift visibly underperforms the other two, and it is read as a supervisor or people problem rather than as a data problem.

108. [manual-adjustment-destroys-evidence] MECHANISM: Bad location mapping, merged SKUs and radio-induced duplicate scans present at the pick face as one identical symptom — system quantity does not match physical quantity — and the single response that fits all three is a manual adjustment. CONSEQUENCE: Each adjustment erases the evidence that would have separated the three causes and fixes the symptom at one location while the underlying defect keeps producing new ones elsewhere.

109. [unpaired-adjustments-degrade-below-legacy] MECHANISM: Correcting a wrong bin properly is a transfer, but under peak pressure by people with half a day's training it becomes a pair of unrelated adjustments, and often only half the pair because the picker is already late. CONSEQUENCE: Within a fortnight the platform accumulates phantom quantity, missing quantity, negative on-hand and reason-code-less adjustments, and its stock position becomes worse than the legacy system's was — from the uncontrolled repair rather than from the migration.

110. [override-habit-persists] MECHANISM: Weeks of heavy manual adjustment teach forty people that the system is wrong and that the correct response is to override it. CONSEQUENCE: The habit outlives the incident and permanently caps the site's inventory accuracy long after the underlying data is fixed.

111. [manual-keying-reintroduces-free-text] MECHANISM: The natural remedy for scans that fail or locations that are wrong is to let people key locations by hand instead of scanning. CONSEQUENCE: Free-text location strings written under time pressure flow back into the platform's clean three-field model, so the data model degrades toward the legacy one from the inside — the exact thing the migration was for.

112. [legacy-box-becomes-location-index] MECHANISM: When neither picker nor supervisor can find the stock, the supervisor walks to the corner office and queries the green screen, which is powered on and perfectly readable. CONSEQUENCE: The frozen legacy system becomes the site's real location index, consulted dozens of times an hour and drivable by three people, and an informal process grows around it that is still in daily use months later.

113. [read-only-not-actually-implemented] MECHANISM: Genuine read-only mode breaks green-screen applications that write session, lock and audit rows on login, so the realistic options are to leave the box writable or to make it unusable, and nobody has decided which. CONSEQUENCE: Either the lookup facility the whole Week 6 arrangement exists to provide does not work, or a fully functional second system sits powered on in the corner office with no technical enforcement and no reconciliation process.

114. [shadow-process-on-the-veterans-shifts] MECHANISM: At four in the morning during peak week, with a customer order blocked and vans queuing, a fifteen-year supervisor's instinct is to solve the customer's problem with the tool he trusts, which is switched on and works. CONSEQUENCE: A shadow process runs on two of three shifts within days, nobody tells the engineers, the two systems diverge again with no reconciliation, and processes started during peak do not stop when peak ends.

115. [paper-improvisation-never-reconciled] MECHANISM: There is no documented answer to what forty people do if the platform is unusable at 06:00, so it is invented on the spot by whichever supervisor is on duty, and veterans know exactly how to run the warehouse on paper. CONSEQUENCE: It keeps the vans moving on the day and is a catastrophe afterwards, because paper taken during peak gets reconciled late, partially, or never.

116. [history-never-migrates] MECHANISM: Only current stock crosses; transaction history, receipt history, lot traceability, cost layers and price history stay on the box, and the plan contains no archive, dump or retention step. CONSEQUENCE: Every historical question — an audit, a recall, a customer dispute, a valuation query — requires the box, so "powered on for a week" becomes permanent for an unpatched, unbacked-up, unmaintained machine, the stated benefit of retiring the legacy stack is never realised, and when it eventually dies the records go with it.

117. [engineers-exhausted-and-unlogged] MECHANISM: The same two people work a full Friday, the cutover night into the early hours, the Saturday count, and then five days of hypercare from Monday 06:00 in peak week, with no relief shift and no handover. CONSEQUENCE: Judgement degrades exactly where the plan concentrates its decisions, and hypercare becomes tired people making unlogged data fixes directly in production, manufacturing next month's discrepancies.

118. [hypercare-is-office-hours-only] MECHANISM: "Both engineers on site all week" means days; two people cannot cover three shifts for five days. CONSEQUENCE: The night shift's first unsupported run on a five-day-old system happens at 22:00 on go-live Monday with nobody to call, and problems found at 02:00 are worked around rather than reported, so the workaround becomes the permanent process.

119. [engineers-become-helpdesk] MECHANISM: Forty people with a system they saw for half a day and two visible experts in the building means every question routes to the engineers, and the cut second training session guarantees that every exception is a question. CONSEQUENCE: The only expert capacity in the plan is spent answering the same procedural questions instead of fixing root causes, so the one thing hypercare existed for is the one thing it does not get.

120. [two-symptoms-cannot-be-told-apart] MECHANISM: A picker failing at the shelf can mean the record is wrong or that the person is doing it wrong because their training is ten days stale, and neither the supervisor nor the picker can distinguish them. CONSEQUENCE: The engineers' scarce days are consumed triaging case by case, and the natural reading from the floor — that people need more training — leads to a response that does not help and then reads as staff resistance.

121. [hypercare-ends-by-calendar] MECHANISM: Hypercare ends on Friday 14 November because the plan says six weeks, not because any criterion has been met, and there is no Week 7 in the document. CONSEQUENCE: The engineers leave a five-day-old system with peak's heaviest weeks still ahead, untrained seasonal staff arriving, and the contractor's engagement over.

122. [escalation-comes-from-customers] MECHANISM: Nothing in the project's own reporting chain can raise a flag — the gate passed by not being defined, the count passed by being too small, the deadline slipped in a footnote — so the first visible signal outside the warehouse is late dispatches, short shipments and back-orders. CONSEQUENCE: Senior escalation happens in peak week, triggered by missed orders and framed as "the new system doesn't work", which is the least analytical frame available.

123. [rollback-demanded-in-a-corridor] MECHANISM: The site manager can demand a return to the old system because the legacy box is visibly powered on, and there is no pre-agreed threshold, no rollback definition, no criteria and no named owner of that decision. CONSEQUENCE: The most consequential decision of the project is negotiated in a corridor during peak week between people who report up different lines, and escalates to a shared boss hearing about all of it for the first time.

124. [site-manager-committed-nothing] MECHANISM: The site manager agreed a constraint, not a resource plan — floor cover for six training sessions, supervisor release for the data work, a night-shift stand-down, relabelling labour, hypercare headcount and agency training were never asked for or agreed. CONSEQUENCE: Each ask arrives later, individually, as a surprise from a project that is behind, and can be declined with perfect reasonableness, so from Week 4 onward every conflict between project need and peak readiness resolves in favour of peak — recorded not as risk decisions but as sensible cost control.

125. [ops-lead-reports-softly] MECHANISM: The person who wrote the plan is simultaneously doing the work, tracking the schedule and reporting the slippage to the site manager. CONSEQUENCE: Reporting biases consistently toward "mostly done, finishing Monday" and "mostly re-keying noise", so by the time the picture is undeniable it is Week 5 and there are no options left.

126. [contractor-latency] MECHANISM: "Available for questions but on another engagement" means asynchronous, best-effort, unpaid help on someone else's priority list, with round trips of hours to a day. CONSEQUENCE: In a three-day survey a single overnight round trip burns a third of the visit, and a question asked on a Thursday afternoon costs the project a weekend.

127. [question-quality-worst-when-access-best] MECHANISM: In Week 1 the engineers are on site but do not yet know enough to ask precise questions; by Week 2, when they have specific answerable ones, they are remote and the contractor's other engagement has ramped up. CONSEQUENCE: The site's technical archaeology is never transferred, and the questions that would have mattered most are asked at the moment they are hardest to get answered.

128. [contractor-incentives-point-away] MECHANISM: The project terminates the contractor's quarterly retainer, so he is being asked to help decommission his own income stream, unbilled, while working for a paying client. CONSEQUENCE: Not sabotage but something more corrosive — terse answers, slow answers, exactly what was asked and nothing volunteered, so the warnings nobody knew to ask for never arrive.

129. [contractor-absent-on-the-night] MECHANISM: Nobody has arranged a paid on-call, an agreed rate, a phone number or a named backup for the cutover window, and getting him on site urgently turns out to cost money and require notice nobody has. CONSEQUENCE: An Oracle-side problem during the freeze has nobody on the planet available to diagnose it, which is the single most likely cause of the cutover overrunning or aborting.

130. [no-owner-for-weeks-two-to-five] MECHANISM: The plan places people only in Week 1 and Week 6 and is silent about Weeks 2, 3, 4 and 5 — the cleanup, the parallel run, the training and the cutover itself — so it never says who is in the building on cutover night. CONSEQUENCE: The four weeks containing all the actual work are done part-time and remotely around other assignments, and the cutover is worked by fewer and more junior people than anyone assumed, because everyone assumed someone else.

131. [bus-factor-of-one-on-the-data] MECHANISM: Two engineers facing this scope split rather than pair — one takes data, one takes devices and printers — and the mapping rules, dedupe decisions and hand-fix reasoning exist only as edits in one person's head. CONSEQUENCE: A single absence in Week 5 makes the cutover impossible to perform, because nobody else can reproduce the transformations.

132. [four-migrated-sites-unused] MECHANISM: Four sites have already been migrated onto this platform, and the plan cites them only as evidence that the software works — nobody from them is on the team, and none of their location-mapping approach, load scripts, printer templates, symbology settings or go-live failure lists are reused. CONSEQUENCE: Northfield reinvents an approach the organisation already owns and re-meets failures it has already met four times.

133. [northfield-is-the-outlier] MECHANISM: Northfield is the last site to move, and whatever made it last — unusual racking, non-standard units, more manual process — is exactly what a platform configuration shaped by the other four does not cover, which is the same fact as the note that no test environment mirrors its layout. CONSEQUENCE: The mismatches surface as "the platform can't do it the way we do it", first in training and then continuously from go-live.

134. [per-site-config-unbudgeted] MECHANISM: Zones, location types, pick-path strategy, replenishment and wave rules, put-away and allocation strategy, user roles, device profiles and printer definitions are all per-site build work, and the plan budgets one week for "data cleanup" and implicitly assumes the platform is otherwise ready. CONSEQUENCE: Northfield is an empty site rather than a configured one, and the missing configuration is discovered as a series of blocking surprises inside weeks that had no room for it.

135. [integrations-never-mentioned] MECHANISM: The legacy box exchanges data with other systems — order intake, supplier ASNs, carrier manifests, finance postings — and no week of the plan mentions a single interface, though every one must be re-pointed on the same Monday and every one has a counterparty who must be told. CONSEQUENCE: Orders, inbound notifications and the end-of-day feeds break on go-live day, and in the worst case the platform holds perfect stock data and no orders to pick against.

136. [valuation-journal-surprises-finance] MECHANISM: Loading opening stock for a new site posts a large inventory journal, and the loaded position embeds the dedupe's discarded quantities, the load's dropped rows and a globally defaulted unit of measure. CONSEQUENCE: The month-end after cutover shows an inventory value that moved for reasons nobody can explain, which surfaces as an unexplained write-off and draws attention from people who were never in the room for any of this.

137. [accuracy-needs-a-count-peak-forbids-it] MECHANISM: Record accuracy does not self-heal; the only genuine repair is a full physical count, which needs a shutdown the business will not authorise during peak. CONSEQUENCE: The site runs the whole of peak on degraded data, absorbed as overtime, expedited freight and manual checking of outbound, with an emergency partial count of the worst zones over a weekend in late November at many times the cost of the check it was substituting for.

138. [mispicks-return-as-claims] MECHANISM: Mis-picks and short-picks ship and come back as claims and wrong-delivery returns three to ten days later, into a returns process nobody was trained on and that the parallel run almost certainly never keyed. CONSEQUENCE: Unbooked returns pile up in a corner and stock accuracy degrades further at the busiest point of peak.

139. [veteran-supervisor-attrition] MECHANISM: The two fifteen-year supervisors carry the site through go-live and peak on personal knowledge, becoming load-bearing infrastructure at the moment they are most tired and least consulted. CONSEQUENCE: If one leaves or goes off sick, the site loses the only surviving copy of the racking map the data got wrong — knowledge the project could have documented and instead scoped as three days of interviews and discarded.

140. [no-data-owner-after-hypercare] MECHANISM: Inventory accuracy on the new platform is a standing process — cycle counting — and the plan establishes none, names no post-hypercare data owner, and sets no retirement date or owner for the legacy box. CONSEQUENCE: After 14 November nobody is responsible for finding or fixing the residual errors, so they are discovered one at a time by pickers and adjusted away rather than corrected at source.

141. [project-recorded-as-delivered] MECHANISM: The site is running on the platform from 10 November, so the project is reported as delivered in November, while actual completion — accuracy restored, staff competent, legacy retired — is a quarter or more away. CONSEQUENCE: The additional cost is booked as peak-season overtime and never attributed back to the plan, so nothing is learned and the same plan is written again for the next site.
