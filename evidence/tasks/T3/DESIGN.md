# Design brief — rooftop rainwater irrigation for the community garden

We want to water 40 raised beds from rainwater collected off the hall roof, with no mains
electricity at the garden.

**Collection.** The hall roof is 180 m2 of corrugated steel, pitched, with gutters on both
long sides feeding two downpipes. Local rainfall averages 60 mm/month, concentrated in
about eight rain days.

**Storage.** Two 1,000-litre IBC totes (the plastic cubes in steel cages), sitting side by
side on a platform of concrete blocks 1.2 m high, connected to each other near the bottom
with a 40 mm hose and a pair of tank connectors so they fill and drain together. Each
downpipe feeds one tote through a leaf strainer. An overflow at the top of each tote runs
to the existing storm drain.

**Distribution.** From a tap at the bottom of tote 1, a 25 mm polythene main runs 35 m
along the garden's north edge. Off it, eight 16 mm lateral lines run down the beds, each
with a manual ball valve at the tee. Each lateral carries twelve 2 litre/hour pressure-
compensating drippers, spaced 40 cm apart.

**Control.** A battery-powered timer valve on the main, set to open for one hour at 6 am
daily. It is the kind that screws onto a garden tap and takes two AA cells.

**Use.** Gardeners open and close the ball valves themselves depending on what is planted
where. In summer the beds need roughly 4 litres per bed per day.
