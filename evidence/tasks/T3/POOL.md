# POOL — distinct verified claims about what happens when this is built and used

1. [roof-yield-exceeds-demand] MECHANISM: 180 m2 of steel roof at a 0.85 runoff coefficient yields about 153 L per mm of rain, ~110,000 L/year, against an annual garden demand of ~22,000-30,000 L. CONSEQUENCE: supply is never the binding constraint and roughly 80% of what is caught (~85,000 L/year) leaves through the overflow, so every water shortage the garden experiences is a buffer problem, not a rainfall problem.

2. [store-refills-in-two-rain-days] MECHANISM: 13 mm of rain — about 1.7 average rain days — refills the entire 2,000 L store from empty. CONSEQUENCE: recovery is near-instant after any rain, so the pattern gardeners actually experience is roughly a week of water after each rain day and then nothing, rather than a gradual seasonal decline.

3. [twelve-day-autonomy] MECHANISM: 2,000 L nominal (about 1,850-1,880 usable) against 160 L/day is roughly twelve days of autonomy, while a climate delivering rain on eight days a month routinely produces 10-21 day gaps. CONSEQUENCE: the store empties in the middle of one to three dry spells every summer, at the point of maximum plant stress, and stays empty until the next rain.

4. [seasonal-rainfall-unstated] MECHANISM: the brief gives a monthly average, not a distribution, and 60 mm/month is equally consistent with a wet-winter/dry-summer climate in which summer months deliver 10-20 mm. CONSEQUENCE: in that reading the tanks fill in April, run dry in the third week of June and supply roughly a third of demand for the rest of the season, i.e. the system does almost nothing in the season it exists for.

5. [downpipe-geometry-contradiction] MECHANISM: gutters on both long sides feed two downpipes at opposite ends of a cross-section through the hall, while the two totes are specified side by side with one downpipe each, which requires a 10-20 m run of pipe that the brief does not contain. CONSEQUENCE: the default build-day resolution is to connect only the near downpipe, halving catchment to 90 m2 and about 4,590 L/month, which removes the surplus against a 4,800 L/month summer demand and puts the system into deficit even in the average case.

6. [gutters-at-capacity] MECHANISM: an ~18 m gutter run with a single end outlet passes roughly 1.0-1.5 L/s against 1.25 L/s off 90 m2 at 50 mm/h, and the new strainer adds head loss at the downpipe entry that was not there before. CONSEQUENCE: heavy storms produce a curtain of water off the gutter at the corner furthest from the downpipe onto the hall wall, and a pre-existing gutter limitation gets attributed to the rainwater project.

7. [strainer-access-2.4m] MECHANISM: the tote inlets sit at about 2.4 m (1.2 m platform plus 1.16 m tote), so clearing a leaf strainer is a ladder job directly above a full tank, on soft ground, in autumn. CONSEQUENCE: the strainers are not cleaned, they blind with moss, grit and leaf mat, and a blinded strainer diverts water rather than filtering it, taking real capture well below the 0.85 the budget assumes.

8. [strainer-failure-invisible] MECHANISM: strainers blind in autumn, when the tanks are full and nothing is being drawn. CONSEQUENCE: the fault produces no symptom for months; the store simply starts the following spring low or empty, and the shortfall is attributed to "we did not get much rain".

9. [interconnect-masks-blocked-strainer] MECHANISM: the totes share a common level through the 40 mm bottom hose, so a tote whose own downpipe is blocked still fills from the other one. CONSEQUENCE: a completely blocked strainer presents as both tanks filling slowly rather than one tank staying empty, removing the one observable that would localise the fault.

10. [interconnect-works] MECHANISM: 40 mm of bore passes roughly 28-45 L/min at only a few centimetres of level difference, more than any credible imbalance between the two downpipes. CONSEQUENCE: the totes genuinely do fill and drain together as the brief claims, and the link also cancels the wind-driven catchment asymmetry between the two roof slopes.

11. [no-isolation-valves] MECHANISM: there are no valves at either end of the interconnect, so the two totes are permanently a single vessel with no partial-failure mode. CONSEQUENCE: any one puncture, weeping tank connector or split hose drains the whole store, and neither tote can be serviced nor the link drained for winter without dumping 2,000 L that took months to collect.

12. [connector-height-strands-water] MECHANISM: a tank connector cannot be fitted at true floor level because the bottle wall curves into the base and the cage's bottom rail blocks the backnut, so the link ends up 100-300 mm up the wall while the only tap is on tote 1. CONSEQUENCE: once the shared level falls to the link, tote 2's remaining 100-300 L can never be drawn, never turns over, and becomes a permanently isolated sump.

13. [dead-volume-below-outlet] MECHANISM: the IBC outlet bore sits 50-100 mm above the internal floor. CONSEQUENCE: 30-80 L per tote is unusable, and the last water drawn in a drought is simultaneously the lowest-pressure and the dirtiest in the tank.

14. [no-first-flush] MECHANISM: nothing in the design discards the first, dirtiest water off the roof, so every event delivers accumulated bird droppings, grit and pollen straight into the tote. CONSEQUENCE: 1-5 kg a year of sediment builds a sludge layer in the bottom 20-40 mm, which is exactly the layer the bottom tap and the interconnect draw from.

15. [sludge-is-permanent] MECHANISM: removing that sludge means draining 1,000 L and reaching through a 150 mm lid at 2.4 m from a ladder, and a full IBC cannot be lifted down. CONSEQUENCE: the annual clean-out never happens, so the tank bottom is a sediment trap for the life of the installation and any filtration has to be downstream rather than upstream.

16. [algae-certain] MECHANISM: translucent HDPE plus nutrient-rich roof water plus an unshaded tank on a platform in full sun produces a visible bloom within 3-8 warm weeks and a wall biofilm that sloughs in sheets on every drawdown and refill. CONSEQUENCE: the store becomes a continuous generator of the exact particulate that plugs sub-millimetre emitter labyrinths downstream.

17. [opaque-cover-kills-gauge] MECHANISM: the translucent wall is the design's only level indicator, and both the algae itself and the obvious remedy for it (painting or wrapping the tote) make the wall opaque. CONSEQUENCE: the single number that would let anyone ration water or tell an empty tank from any other fault disappears during the first summer, which is the season it matters.

18. [mosquito-breeding] MECHANISM: an unscreened inlet, lid and overflow open onto 2,000 L of warm, still, nutrient-rich water on a 7-10 day Culex cycle. CONSEQUENCE: larvae are established by midsummer and the issue arrives as a complaint from neighbours or the hall committee — a reputational problem for the garden rather than a maintenance ticket.

19. [overflow-undersized] MECHANISM: with the downpipe fully diverted into the tote, the tote's overflow is the only exit for the entire storm flow off 90 m2 — up to 75 L/min at 50 mm/h — while a typical 25-32 mm tank-connector overflow passes 15-30 L/min. CONSEQUENCE: the tote surcharges in every heavy storm, and because the tanks are full for most of the year this is the normal operating condition rather than a rare edge case.

20. [surcharge-down-the-face] MECHANISM: when the overflow cannot keep up, water rises above the port and escapes at whatever opening is lowest — the lid seal or the strainer housing. CONSEQUENCE: roughly 50 L/min pours down the outside of the tanks from 2.4 m onto the block platform and the ground at the base of the piers for the duration of every storm.

21. [surcharge-up-the-downpipe] MECHANISM: if the lid and inlet are sealed instead, the backup has nowhere to go but up the downpipe until the water column reaches the gutter. CONSEQUENCE: the gutter floods along the hall, water runs down the wall, damp appears inside weeks later, and the hall committee becomes a second stakeholder in the garden's irrigation.

22. [diverter-disconnected-in-autumn] MECHANISM: the cheapest response to a hall-damp complaint is to disconnect the downpipe from the tote "for now", and it is always made in autumn or winter when the tanks are full anyway. CONSEQUENCE: nobody remembers to reconnect it before the dry season, so year two starts the summer with a store that never refilled.

23. [platform-lateral-instability] MECHANISM: 2,130 kg sits on dry-stacked, unmortared, unbonded, uncapped block piers 1.2 m high with no tension capacity, exposed to ladder thrust from strainer cleaning, wheelbarrows, and children treating it as a climbing frame. CONSEQUENCE: resistance to a sideways shove is friction alone, and one shifted course is not a wobble but two tonnes of water coming off a 1.2 m stack.

24. [blocks-are-not-the-question] MECHANISM: bearing on the blockwork is about 27 kPa against a block compressive strength of 7 MPa or more. CONSEQUENCE: anyone who sanity-checks the platform by asking "will the blocks take the weight" gets a reassuring answer and has checked the wrong thing, leaving the load path and the ground unexamined.

25. [soil-bearing-settlement] MECHANISM: the load reaches the ground through roughly 0.76 m2 of block bearing at about 28 kPa in eight small patches, on cultivated garden topsoil with no footing, and it peaks from November to March when the store is full, undrawn and the ground is at its softest. CONSEQUENCE: differential settlement and a visible lean develop over the first wet winter or two, and the usual remedy is a slate shimmed under one corner, which corrects the appearance and none of the mechanism.

26. [bladder-needs-full-support] MECHANISM: an IBC bottle is only structurally sound when its pallet bears flat across its full area, and the natural DIY platform — block walls under the pallet edges with a void in the middle — lets the bottle sag into the gap. CONSEQUENCE: the HDPE creeps at the base corner radius under sustained load and eventually splits, releasing 1,000 L in under a minute from 1.2 m up.

27. [leaks-converge-on-piers] MECHANISM: the overflow spill, the strainer overspill, a weeping thread at the tap and a split interconnect all discharge at the same place, the base of the platform. CONSEQUENCE: the system's own failure modes repeatedly saturate its bearing soil, and settlement, tilt, stressed connectors and a worsening leak close into a feedback loop in which the small unnoticed drip is the start of the two-tonne failure.

28. [interconnect-freezes-first] MECHANISM: the 40 mm link is a small, permanently full, unlagged water column outside the thermal mass of either tote, sagging below both tank floors, and it cannot be drained without emptying the store. CONSEQUENCE: it splits on the first hard freezing night while the 1,000 L bottles survive, and on thaw the entire store discharges at ground level directly under the platform.

29. [winter-discovery-in-spring] MECHANISM: every freeze failure happens with nobody at the site and no water is drawn again until spring. CONSEQUENCE: the fault surfaces in April as a tap that runs air or a store that quietly emptied in January, half a year after the cause, at the worst possible moment in the growing season.

30. [pc-drippers-below-band] MECHANISM: the emitters see roughly 0.08-0.20 bar (water surface about 2.3 m, emitters on 0.4 m raised beds) against a pressure-compensating regulating range that begins near 0.5 bar. CONSEQUENCE: the diaphragm never engages and each dripper behaves as a bare labyrinth passing roughly 0.6-1.3 L/h instead of 2 L/h, so the one-hour run delivers about 60-130 L instead of 192 L.

31. [compensation-unavailable] MECHANISM: below the regulating band flow follows roughly the square root of pressure and the diaphragm rests in an unrepeatable position, widening between-emitter scatter from a 3-5% coefficient of variation to tens of percent. CONSEQUENCE: the uniformity the premium component was bought for is not merely absent but worse than a cheap non-compensating turbulent emitter would have given at the same head.

32. [cnl-variant-delivers-nothing] MECHANISM: anti-drain (CNL) versions of the same emitter sit on the shelf at almost the same price and carry a check-valve cracking pressure of 0.15-0.3 bar, which lands squarely inside the available head. CONSEQUENCE: if those were bought, whole laterals open erratically or not at all and the system can deliver essentially nothing from day one, with the symptom varying by position and by morning for no visible reason.

33. [output-tracks-tank-level] MECHANISM: with compensation inactive, emitter output follows the falling water surface as the store draws down. CONSEQUENCE: output drops roughly 25-35% from full tank to nearly empty, so the system waters least well at the end of a dry spell, which is exactly when the plants need it most.

34. [head-cannot-be-raised] MECHANISM: reaching 0.5 bar requires about 5 m of water column, and an extra course of blocks adds 0.2 m, which is +17% head and, since out-of-band flow scales with the square root of head, about +8% water. CONSEQUENCE: the obvious physical fix the group will try is undetectable with a jam jar, and a day of block-laying that visibly changes nothing corrodes belief that the system is fixable at all.

35. [raised-beds-eat-the-budget] MECHANISM: head is measured from the water surface to the emitter outlet, and the emitters lie on raised beds roughly 0.4 m above grade — a term the brief never states. CONSEQUENCE: about a fifth of the entire head budget is consumed by bed height before any component is considered, and a taller bed takes proportionally more.

36. [friction-is-negligible] MECHANISM: 192 L/h through 35 m of 21 mm main costs about 7 cm of head and a 16 mm lateral costs millimetres, against 130-190 cm available. CONSEQUENCE: the pipework is blameless and generously sized, so upsizing, shortening or "improving the flow" of the pipes changes nothing measurable and every hour spent on them is wasted.

37. [elevation-sets-everything] MECHANISM: because friction is negligible, each emitter's pressure is set almost entirely by how far it sits below the water surface. CONSEQUENCE: any fall across the garden produces a permanent lush-at-the-bottom, dry-at-the-top gradient down every row that gardeners will attribute to soil, and any lateral running up a rise gets little or nothing at all.

38. [valve-state-does-not-couple] MECHANISM: closing seven of the eight ball valves changes the head at the remaining emitters by only a few percent, because friction is about 3% of the head budget. CONSEQUENCE: the folk remedy of shutting other people's valves to get more water to your own bed delivers no benefit whatsoever to the person doing it while removing supply entirely from the beds shut off, and nothing observable in the garden can falsify the theory.

39. [main-is-only-oversized-at-a-trickle] MECHANISM: friction scales with roughly the square of flow, so at flushing or open-end flows of several hundred L/h the same main costs half a metre or more of a 1.8 m budget. CONSEQUENCE: laterals can only be flushed one at a time, and nobody can fill a can or run a hose from the far end of the 35 m main — the tap at the tote stays the garden's only real water point, 35 m from half the beds.

40. [coverage-arithmetic-fails] MECHANISM: twelve drippers at 40 cm spacing occupy 4.4 m of run, so eight laterals provide about 35 m of emitter line in total for 40 beds that need several times that. CONSEQUENCE: between half and three-quarters of the beds have no dripline at all, and the failure is invisible on paper because the water sum (96 x 2 L/h x 1 h = 192 L = 4.8 L/bed against a 4 L target) is internally correct.

41. [fixing-coverage-empties-store] MECHANISM: extending dripline to all 40 beds multiplies the emitter count and therefore the daily draw by about 2.5 times. CONSEQUENCE: the store's autonomy collapses from about twelve days to under four, revealing that the water budget only ever balanced because the pipework reached a fifth of the garden.

42. [wetting-bulbs-do-not-merge] MECHANISM: a point emitter in free-draining raised-bed mix wets a bulb only 15-30 cm across, and at 40 cm spacing — and at the sub-litre volumes actually delivered — adjacent bulbs do not overlap. CONSEQUENCE: each irrigated bed gets two or three damp coins with dry mix between them, so broadcast sowings and every spring seedbed get nothing and the watering cans come out regardless.

43. [demand-target-is-low] MECHANISM: 4 L/day spread over a standard 1.2 x 2.4 m raised bed is 1.4 mm/day, against a summer evapotranspiration demand of 4-6 mm/day. CONSEQUENCE: unless the beds are much smaller than standard, even a perfectly functioning system is a supplement rather than the garden's water supply, and the resulting shortfall will be blamed on the drippers rather than on the target.

44. [no-filter-anywhere] MECHANISM: nothing stands between algal floc, biofilm sheets and roof grit in the store and 96 emitters whose labyrinths are 0.5-0.7 mm and whose manufacturers specify 120 mesh as a minimum. CONSEQUENCE: emitters begin plugging at about week six and 20-50% are dead by the end of the first summer, in a random patchwork that presents as individual plants dying for no visible reason.

45. [no-scouring-velocity] MECHANISM: lateral velocity is around 0.05 m/s against the 0.3-0.6 m/s needed to move sediment, and no lateral has an openable end or flush valve. CONSEQUENCE: whatever enters a lateral stays and packs at the closed end, so the last two or three drippers on every lateral plug first — a spatial pattern legible in the planting.

46. [pc-emitters-not-cleanable] MECHANISM: a plugged PC emitter cannot be blown clear at 0.1 bar, and pin-cleaning destroys the labyrinth ridges or punctures the diaphragm. CONSEQUENCE: each "repaired" dripper becomes a free-running hole, and any dripper pulled out and not reseated leaves an unpluggable hole in the tube that quietly dribbles the store away overnight.

47. [nightly-drain-down-floods-one-bed] MECHANISM: the 17-21 L standing in the main and laterals drains out of whichever emitters sit lowest every time the timer closes, because non-check-valve emitters do not seal. CONSEQUENCE: one bed receives four or five beds' entire daily ration in a single dump every morning — 350-700 L over a season into one spot — producing waterlogging and root rot in a garden that is otherwise short of water.

48. [refill-penalty-hits-far-laterals] MECHANISM: every run must first re-charge those 17-21 L before the far end sees any pressure, which at the real flow takes 10-18 minutes of the 60-minute window. CONSEQUENCE: the far laterals lose a quarter to a third of their run time, systematically, the same beds every day, on top of already having the least head.

49. [drain-down-sucks-soil-in] MECHANISM: as the lines empty each morning the emitters at the high end go into slight suction and draw wet soil and fine roots back in through the outlet. CONSEQUENCE: a one-way clogging mechanism runs every night on the high emitters of every lateral, independent of water quality and unaffected by any upstream filter.

50. [air-locks-are-permanent] MECHANISM: at 0.05-0.16 m/s the flow cannot sweep a bubble past a high point (0.3-0.6 m/s is needed) and there is no air bleed or requirement for continuous fall. CONSEQUENCE: air pockets in a surface-laid 35 m main are permanent, and whatever lies beyond one receives nothing indefinitely, for a reason that cannot be found by looking at the pipe.

51. [thermal-snaking-creates-high-points] MECHANISM: 35 m of surface-laid polythene moves about 200 mm in length over a 30 C seasonal swing. CONSEQUENCE: the main snakes into new high points that feed the permanent air locks and works push-fit barb joints loose over a couple of seasons.

52. [open-path-empties-store] MECHANISM: a trodden barbed ball valve, a spade or strimmer strike, or a tap left open creates an unrestricted path passing roughly 1,000 L/h at this head — about five times the system's whole design flow. CONSEQUENCE: the entire usable store is on the ground within about two hours, unattended, and nothing in the design bounds it: no non-return valve, no flow limiter, no float switch and no alarm.

53. [low-pressure-leaks-seep] MECHANISM: at 0.1-0.2 bar every pipe, tee, barb and valve is at 1-2% of its rating, so nothing bursts and nothing sprays. CONSEQUENCE: damage never announces itself — a struck main or a hand-tightened thread weeps a few litres a day silently at the base of the platform and is discovered as an empty tank rather than as a leak.

54. [no-shutoff-upstream-of-the-adapters] MECHANISM: the only isolation is the IBC's own valve, buried under an S60x6-to-3/4in adapter stack that the rigid 25 mm main cantilevers off. CONSEQUENCE: if the adapter chain or the valve flange is what fails there is nothing left to close, and a foot caught in the main can crack a flange that cannot be repaired, taking the tote with it.

55. [diaphragm-timer-never-opens] MECHANISM: the common pilot-operated diaphragm hose timer uses line pressure itself to lift the main diaphragm and specifies a minimum operating pressure of about 0.5 bar, against the 0.10-0.19 bar available at the tap. CONSEQUENCE: the solenoid latches audibly, the display reports the zone as running, and no water passes at all — a control system that reports success and delivers nothing.

56. [diaphragm-timer-will-not-reseat] MECHANISM: the same valve needs differential pressure to force its diaphragm back onto the seat, so at 0.15 bar it can stay cracked open after the cycle ends. CONSEQUENCE: the store drains continuously through the emitters at tens of litres an hour and is gone in a day or two, presenting as one saturated patch and a tank reading zero.

57. [timer-type-is-a-coin-flip] MECHANISM: the brief specifies the form factor ("screws onto a garden tap, two AA cells") but not the mechanism, and the motorised ball-valve version of that same form factor has no minimum pressure and works fine here. CONSEQUENCE: whether anything at all happens at 6 am is decided by which of two visually identical products was bought, and the replacement bought after the first failure is usually the same type, which fails identically.

58. [batteries-fail-silently] MECHANISM: two AA cells drive 730 actuations a year plus an LCD in an outdoor enclosure through a winter that halves alkaline capacity, and most units fail in their last state and lose their program on a battery change. CONSEQUENCE: irrigation stops on a random morning with no announcement, and every battery change is a fresh chance to leave the garden unwatered until somebody works out why.

59. [open-loop-scheduling] MECHANISM: the timer has no rain skip, no soil sensor and no seasonal schedule, so it opens for an hour every morning including the day after 20 mm of rain and every day through winter. CONSEQUENCE: dormant beds are watered into saturation and the lines are held full of water into the frost season, until a human switches it off in December — and that human does not switch it back on in spring.

60. [frost-splits-charged-components] MECHANISM: the timer body, eight ball valves and 96 emitter labyrinths hold trapped water with no drain-down procedure, no low-point drain and no way to lift the laterals. CONSEQUENCE: the plastic timer body and two or three valves crack in the first hard freeze, and they are found in April as a leak somewhere along 35 m of pipe under last season's dead growth.

61. [split-emitters-starve-the-far-end] MECHANISM: frost-split emitters pass far more than intact ones, and once total flow reaches several hundred litres an hour the main's friction — negligible at design flow — becomes the dominant term. CONSEQUENCE: in spring the beds nearest the tank are soaked while the far laterals get nothing, and the gardener concludes the far end is blocked and spends the season working on the wrong end of the pipe.

62. [six-am-run-has-no-witness] MECHANISM: the only event in the entire system happens at 06:00, before anyone is on site, and an hour's output at these flows has dried to a damp coin by mid-morning. CONSEQUENCE: no failure announces itself; the first signal of any fault is plant stress a week or two later, by which time a dozen competing explanations are available.

63. [one-symptom-many-causes] MECHANISM: an empty tank, a timer that never opened, dead batteries, plugged emitters, an air lock and a valve closed by somebody else all present identically as dry soil at 3 pm, and the design contains no level gauge, flow meter, run indicator, pressure gauge or test port. CONSEQUENCE: the garden cannot tell which fault to fix, so it cycles through folk remedies in the wrong order, exhausts its patience, and abandons a system in which each individual fault was cheaply fixable.

64. [spring-hides-the-defect] MECHANISM: the system commissions into a wet spring in which the rain is watering the beds anyway. CONSEQUENCE: the core delivery shortfall produces no symptom at all for six to eight weeks and is first noticed in the first hot dry week of summer, three months after the cause, with maximum anger and minimum information.

65. [longer-run-converts-the-failure] MECHANISM: extending the run from one hour to three is the one intervention available to gardeners that genuinely helps, because out-of-band emitter output is proportional to time, and it roughly triples the daily draw. CONSEQUENCE: autonomy falls by about two-thirds, so a chronic mild underdelivery is converted into an abrupt total failure arriving partway through a drought, when the tanks empty and head — and therefore output — collapses together.

66. [valves-drift-open] MECHANISM: opening your own valve is a private act with an obvious payoff while closing someone else's is a social act with none. CONSEQUENCE: within a few weeks all eight valves are permanently open, the manual zoning the design depends on quietly ceases to exist, and every run waters fallow beds along with planted ones.

67. [valve-state-is-invisible] MECHANISM: the only indicator of a lateral's state is a handle angle on an unlabelled black valve at a tee at ground level, up to 35 m from the beds it controls and under foliage by June. CONSEQUENCE: closures are never reversed and cannot be attributed, so a bed dies weeks after somebody shut its line for a reason that made sense at the time, with no record of who or when.

68. [control-granularity-mismatch] MECHANISM: one global run duration plus eight binary switches is the entire control vocabulary for 40 independently managed beds, five beds to a valve. CONSEQUENCE: a gardener cannot give her own bed more, less or differently timed water without changing four other people's supply, so the design hands an unresolved governance problem to the users where it surfaces as social conflict rather than as an engineering fault.

69. [tap-is-the-real-product] MECHANISM: the tap sits at 1.2-1.35 m — exactly watering-can height — under 1-2 m of head, passing 10-15 L/min through the adapter train and far more through the bare 2 in valve. CONSEQUENCE: a 10 L can fills in well under a minute, hand-watering visibly works where the drip visibly does not, and the tap becomes by a wide margin the most-used function of the whole installation.

70. [timer-blocks-the-only-tap] MECHANISM: there is one tap and the timer is screwed onto it, so filling a can means unscrewing, bypassing or splitting the only outlet. CONSEQUENCE: the timer is left off, cross-threaded or shut at a splitter, and the 6 am run stops happening on a large fraction of mornings for reasons nobody records or notices.

71. [drip-abandoned-tanks-kept] MECHANISM: hand-watering and the drip network compete for the same store, and hand-watering wins because it takes four minutes and visibly wets the plant while the drip takes an hour and shows nothing. CONSEQUENCE: the modal one-year outcome is not collapse but substitution — the dripline stays in the beds as litter, nobody repairs it because it stopped mattering, and the garden ends up with an excellent 2,000 L gravity-fed can-filling station.

72. [no-owner-no-rota] MECHANISM: batteries, strainer clearing, autumn shutdown, spring restart, emitter flushing and platform inspection are every one of them unassigned in a volunteer organisation, and several of them need a ladder. CONSEQUENCE: the system does not fail so much as stop being anybody's job, and year two opens with last autumn's leaves still in the strainers, a dead timer, and the person who built it gone.

73. [mains-water-substitutes] MECHANISM: when the store runs dry in midsummer the gardeners fall back on carrying water or running a hose from the hall's mains tap. CONSEQUENCE: the system fails hardest at exactly the moment it exists to serve, and once the hose is the real supply the rainwater installation becomes decorative while still catching and discarding tens of thousands of litres a year.

74. [food-safety-pathway] MECHANISM: the store receives unfiltered roof runoff carrying bird droppings and, from bare galvanised sheet or old lead-bearing paint, first-flush metals, and the tap draws from the sediment layer where both concentrate. CONSEQUENCE: root-zone drip keeps the exposure low, but the practice that actually emerges — filling cans and splashing them over salad leaves — reinstates a contamination pathway the design has no answer to.

75. [winter-stagnation] MECHANISM: from October to April the store sits full and motionless with essentially no draw, and the stranded bottom layer never moves at all. CONSEQUENCE: the bottom goes anaerobic and the first draw of spring smells of sulphide, prompting the instinct to scrub the tanks out — which means dumping 2,000 L at the start of the growing season.
